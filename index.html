每个条目的 n 是它离最近整数的距离取负对数，即小数点后连续 0 或 9 的位数；complexity 是 RIES 符号权重之和；surplus 大于 0 表示这个巧合比同等复杂度下随机可期的更罕见。

For each entry, n is the negative log of its distance to the nearest integer, i.e. the run of 0s or 9s after the decimal point; complexity is the sum of RIES symbol weights; a surplus above 0 means the coincidence is rarer than chance would produce at that complexity.

定义

质量函数

对一个 almost integer $x$，定义

$$\text{surplus}(x) = n(x) - \frac{\text{complexity}(x)}{10},\qquad n(x) = -\log_{10}\left|x - \operatorname{nint}(x)\right|$$

$n$ 用绝对误差计算，是小数点后连续 0 或 9 位数的连续量版本：$e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}$ 与最近整数相差 $7.5\times10^{-13}$，$n = 12.12$。不采用相对精度，因为一个数的整数部分有多少位与巧合无关：给定量级后，随机实数的小数部分是均匀分布的，落在整数 $\varepsilon$ 邻域内的概率就是 $2\varepsilon$。

复杂度：RIES 的符号权重

complexity 借用 Robert Munafo 的 RIES 中的 complexity() 函数。表达式写成后缀形式，复杂度是每个符号权重之和，权重 = 基数 10 + 符号的个别权重。基数保证每个符号权重为正，搜索才能按复杂度递增终止；它同时让 complexity/10 近似于描述整个表达式所需的十进制位数。

校准

为什么是 n − complexity/10

ries.c 的日志记录了复杂度 $\le 64$ 的表达式共 2 236 462 个，$\log_{10}$ 后为 6.35，约等于 $64/10$。也就是说复杂度 $c$ 对应大约 $10^{c/10}$ 个同样简单的表达式。随机实数落在整数 $10^{-n}$ 邻域内的概率约为 $10^{-n}$，于是在复杂度不超过 $c$ 的表达式中，和某条目一样好的近整数期望有 $10^{c/10-n}$ 个。surplus 就是这个期望个数的负对数：大于 0 说明比随机碰巧更罕见，小于 0 说明这种精度在这个复杂度下本来就会出现。这与 Borwein 讨论"high precision fraud"时的判据一致。

对平凡家族做穷举检验：$\sqrt2\cdot a/b$（$a\le 20000$, $b\le 300$）最好只有 $-3.1$，$\pi\cdot 113 = 354.99997$ 为 $-0.38$，$a/\ln a$（$a \le 10^6$）最好是 $163/\ln 163$ 的 $-2.3$。对 RIES 全空间枚举到复杂度 74（约 197 万个不同值），剔除放大器后没有任何条目 surplus 为正。表中三个正盈余的条目全部来自模函数（$j$ 不变量）。

作为比较，$n/c$ 与 $n/c^2$ 没有概率含义：$n/c$ 把 5 位换 33 的 $\sin 11$ 与 34 位换 93 的嵌套余弦看成只差 2.4 倍，实际稀有程度差 $10^{23}$ 倍；$n/c^2$ 的排名会随 RIES 基数的任意选择而重排。

剥离

放大器

按符号计数的复杂度识别不了两类机械结构，它们能用很低的复杂度换取任意多的精度，因此单独处理：

  • 临界点处的三角函数。$\cos y \approx \pm1$ 等价于 $y \approx k\pi$，而 $\cos(k\pi+\varepsilon) = \pm(1 - \varepsilon^2/2)$，底层近似 $y\approx k\pi$ 的误差被平方。$\cos(\pi\cos(\pi\cos(\ln(\pi+20))))$ 有 34 位，全部由 $e^{\pi} - \pi \approx 20$ 的 3 位机械放大而来。此类条目归并到核心近整数打分。
  • Pisot 数的幂。若代数整数 $x$ 的其余共轭根模都小于 1，则 $x^k$ 与整数的距离按几何级数收缩，而复杂度只随 $\log k$ 增长，surplus 随 $k$ 无界上升。用 findpoly 求底数的最小多项式自动检测，命中者直接退出排名，如 supergolden 数 $\psi^{11}$。

Definition

The quality function

For an almost integer $x$ define

$$\text{surplus}(x) = n(x) - \frac{\text{complexity}(x)}{10},\qquad n(x) = -\log_{10}\left|x - \operatorname{nint}(x)\right|$$

$n$ uses the absolute error and is the continuous version of the run of 0s or 9s after the decimal point: $e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}$ misses the nearest integer by $7.5\times10^{-13}$, so $n = 12.12$. Relative precision is not used, because the number of digits before the decimal point has nothing to do with the coincidence: once the magnitude is fixed, the fractional part of a random real is uniformly distributed, and the probability of landing within $\varepsilon$ of an integer is simply $2\varepsilon$.

Complexity: RIES symbol weights

Complexity is borrowed from the complexity() function in Robert Munafo's RIES. An expression is written in postfix form and its complexity is the sum of the weights of its symbols, where weight = base 10 + the symbol's individual weight. The base keeps every symbol weight positive, so that a search ordered by increasing complexity terminates; it also makes complexity/10 roughly the number of decimal digits needed to describe the whole expression.

A comment in ries.c says constant weights are close to $10\log_{10}x$, and this site extrapolates from it: an integer $N \ge 10$ weighs $10 + \operatorname{round}(10\log_{10}N)$, so 163 costs 32. Symbols RIES lacks are weighted by analogy: $\cosh,\sinh,\tanh$ cost 13 like the trigonometric functions; $\Gamma$, digamma, erfi and $\zeta$ cost 15 like Lambert $W$; Euler's $\gamma$, Catalan's constant, Khinchin's constant and the supergolden ratio $\psi$ cost 18 like $\phi$. Each entry is scored on its cheapest form: $e^{\pi}$ is pi exp (27) rather than e pi ^ (36), and $\pi^4$ is pi sq sq (32).

Calibration

Why n − complexity/10

The ries.c log records 2 236 462 expressions of complexity $\le 64$; $\log_{10}$ of that is 6.35, about $64/10$. So complexity $c$ corresponds to roughly $10^{c/10}$ equally simple expressions. A random real lands within $10^{-n}$ of an integer with probability about $10^{-n}$, so among expressions of complexity at most $c$ one expects $10^{c/10-n}$ almost integers as good as a given entry. The surplus is the negative log of that expected count: above 0 means rarer than chance, below 0 means such precision is bound to appear at that complexity anyway. This is the same test Borwein used when discussing "high precision fraud".

Exhaustive checks on trivial families: $\sqrt2\cdot a/b$ ($a\le 20000$, $b\le 300$) peaks at $-3.1$; $\pi\cdot 113 = 354.99997$ reaches $-0.38$; $a/\ln a$ ($a \le 10^6$) peaks at $163/\ln 163$ with $-2.3$. Enumerating the whole RIES space up to complexity 74 (about 1.97 million distinct values) and removing amplifiers leaves no entry with positive surplus. The three positive entries in the table all come from modular functions (the $j$-invariant).

By comparison, $n/c$ and $n/c^2$ carry no probabilistic meaning: $n/c$ rates $\sin 11$ (5 digits for 33) and the nested cosine (34 digits for 93) as differing by a factor of 2.4, when their rarity differs by $10^{23}$; the ranking under $n/c^2$ reshuffles with the arbitrary choice of the RIES base weight.

Stripping

Amplifiers

A symbol-counting complexity cannot see two mechanical constructions that buy arbitrary precision at almost no cost, so they are handled separately:

  • Trigonometric functions at a critical point. $\cos y \approx \pm1$ is the same as $y \approx k\pi$, and $\cos(k\pi+\varepsilon) = \pm(1 - \varepsilon^2/2)$ squares the error of the underlying approximation $y\approx k\pi$. The 34 digits of $\cos(\pi\cos(\pi\cos(\ln(\pi+20))))$ are all manufactured from the 3 digits of $e^{\pi} - \pi \approx 20$. Such entries are scored on their core almost integer.
  • Powers of Pisot numbers. If an algebraic integer $x$ has all its other conjugates inside the unit circle, then $x^k$ approaches integers geometrically while complexity grows only like $\log k$, so the surplus rises without bound. The base's minimal polynomial is found with findpoly and matching entries are dropped from the ranking, e.g. the supergolden ratio $\psi^{11}$.

Scope

Included and excluded

Entries come from MathWorld's Almost Integer page, including the 13 values of the $e^{\pi\sqrt n}$ table and the Hickerson numbers $h_0$ to $h_{17}$. Excluded are entries requiring infinite sums or products (the Borwein series, the theta sum, the Maze–Minder $u_k$, the q-Pochhammer symbol), non-closed forms (an elliptic integral of the third kind, a Student $t$ quantile) and anything involving physical constants or units. All values were computed with mpmath at 120 digits and agree with the figures MathWorld prints; three formula images on that page have ambiguous or wrong alt text, corrected here by numerical check.

Appendix

RIES built-in symbols

RIES writes every symbol as a single character and an expression as a postfix string of them: pE is $e^{\pi}$, 2q is $\sqrt2$, 5 2 v is $\sqrt[5]{2}$. Symbols fall into three "stack effect types" (seft): constants push a value, unary functions replace the top of the stack, binary operators pop two values and push one.
